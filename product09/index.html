Your Beard Has Changed With Age. Has Your Shaver Kept Up?

Why the stubble that was easy to shave at 30 may be harder to manage at 60—and how a different motor design may help tackle tougher whiskers without adding more blades.

By David Miller, Grooming & Consumer Technology ContributorUpdated: October 2026 | 5-Minute Read

If you've been shaving for 30 or 40 years, you've probably got your routine down. You know how to hold your razor, how much pressure to use, and which spots usually need a second pass.

But lately, you may have noticed something different.

The stubble around your chin and neck seems harder to shave cleanly. What used to take just a few minutes now takes longer. You go over the same spots again and again, and by the time you're done, your neck feels irritated before your day has even started.

You might try a different shaving cream, adjust your technique, or spend more on a new razor. But what if the issue isn't just your shaving routine?

The facial hair you're shaving today may not be quite the same as it was 20 or 30 years ago.

For some men, facial hair becomes coarser or feels more wiry with age. When tougher stubble meets skin that's more prone to irritation, getting a close shave can take more effort—and repeated passes can make the discomfort worse.

That raises a question worth asking: If your shaving needs have changed, could a different approach to shaving help?

Why Shaving Felt Easy at 30, but Feels Like Sandpaper Today

As you get older, a few things can change that make your usual shaving routine less comfortable than it used to be.

Your Neck Skin Becomes More Delicate

With age, skin gradually loses some of its thickness, elasticity, and resilience. The skin around your neck may become more prone to irritation, especially when you repeatedly pass a blade over the same area. Even a small nick can be frustrating, particularly if you take medication that makes bleeding more likely.

Your Stubble Gets Harder to Manage

Gray whiskers don't automatically become thicker or coarser just because they lose pigment, but facial hair can feel stiffer and more wiry with age. Add in hairs that grow in different directions around the neck, and getting a clean shave becomes more challenging. You may find yourself going over the same spots repeatedly, increasing the chance of irritation.

Shaving Takes More Effort Than It Used To

For some men, stiff fingers, reduced dexterity, or changes in eyesight can make a morning shave more awkward. Reaching the curves around the throat while trying to maintain the right angle and pressure takes extra care. What used to be a quick, automatic routine can start to feel like a chore.

More Blades Aren't Always the Answer

If your beard has changed with age, simply adding more blades may not be the answer.

Traditional multi-blade razors rely on repeated contact with the skin, while electric shavers depend on their cutting system to handle the stubble in front of them. When coarse whiskers are harder to cut cleanly, you may find yourself going over the same spots more than you'd like.
